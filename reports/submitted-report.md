Sales Performance & Profitability Dashboard
Three-page dashboard report

Figure 1. Final SAC dashboard for 2019 (Actual).
Dashboard purpose and stakeholder
The page is designed for a sales manager who wants to understand overall profit and then find the regions, customers and products behind the result. It supports the existing case-study pages without copying them. The Overview shows the main results, Customer View gives detailed records, Product View focuses on bicycle sales, and Dynamic Analysis lets users change the measure being studied. My page brings the most useful results together so management can decide what to check or improve first.
Design rationale
The layout follows a simple top-down flow. The two KPIs show whether the company is making a healthy contribution. The country and monthly charts explain where and when the results happened, while the customer and product charts show where action may be needed. I used a light background and consistent country colours to make the page easier to read. The Date filter allows more than one year to be selected, but 2019 is the default so the comparison with the previous year is clear. Sales Organization, Customer and Division filters allow users to focus on a smaller area. I did not add another Country filter because one already exists for the whole story. Linked analysis connects the interactive charts and KPIs across the dashboard. Selecting a country, month, sales organisation, customer or product category updates the related visuals, allowing users to investigate performance from different starting points without manually changing every filter.
Stakeholder value: The page answers five connected questions: Are margins healthy? Where is performance strongest? When does demand peak? Which customers matter most? Which products require intervention?

Why the 46% target matters
A 46% contribution margin means that for every $1 of sales, $0.46 should remain after the costs linked to producing and selling the products. This amount helps to cover fixed costs, such as salaries and rent, before contributing to profit. OpenStax explains that a higher contribution margin reduces the sales needed to cover the same fixed costs and profit target. This makes 46% a useful warning line for Global Bike. The 2019 result of 44.57% is 1.43 percentage points below target. Based on revenue of about $155.92 million, the gap is roughly $2.23 million. Research did not show that 46% is a standard across bicycle companies. Giant Group reported a 21.5% gross margin in 2019, while Accell Group reported a 31.6% added-value ratio. These are different measures because they do not subtract exactly the same costs as contribution margin. Therefore, they cannot be compared directly with Global Bike's 46%, which I treat as an internal case-study target rather than an industry average.

Overall and Regional Performance
Why these visuals were chosen, what they show, and what management can do

Figure 2. Profitability, country performance and monthly contribution by sales organisation.
1. Contribution Margin % KPI
Rationale: This KPI shows whether sales are leaving enough money after the related product costs. This is important because higher revenue does not always mean better performance.
Finding: The 2019 margin is 44.57%. It improved by 8.02% from 2018, but it is still 1.43 percentage points below the case-study target of 46%. The red value makes this gap easy to notice.
Action: Management should check selling prices, discounts, the mix of products sold and product-related costs. The aim is to improve the margin without causing a large drop in sales.
2. Contribution Margin GC KPI
Rationale: This KPI shows the actual amount of contribution earned in dollars. It works with the percentage KPI and avoids repeating the Revenue KPI already shown on the Overview page.
Finding: Global Bike generated $69.48 million in contribution margin in 2019. This is a large amount, but the percentage KPI still shows that the company did not meet its 46% target.
 Action: Use the remaining charts to find the countries and products where margin can be improved the most.
3. Revenue and Profitability by Country
Rationale: The chart compares both revenue and margin for each country. This gives a more complete picture than comparing revenue alone.
Finding: Germany generated about $90.12 million with a 47.92% margin. The US generated $65.79 million with a 39.98% margin. Germany is above the 46% target, while the US is clearly below it.
Action: Maintain Germany’s strong performance and investigate why the US margin is weaker. Management can check US East and US West to see which sales organisation needs more attention.
4. Monthly Revenue Contribution by Sales Organisation
Rationale: The stacked bars show the monthly pattern and how much each sales organisation added to the total. This explains the peak better than the original Overview line chart.
Finding: Revenue rises from April and reaches $33.36 million in June. Germany North contributes $10.82 million, US East $9.66 million, Germany South $9.10 million and US West $3.77 million. Research shows that cycling activity is often higher during summer, so seasonal demand is one possible reason. However, the sales data cannot prove this by itself.
 Action: Prepare stock, staff and sales campaigns before the April-June increase. Compare June across other years and check promotion, weather and stock data before deciding on the exact reason for the peak.
Management priority: The difference between Germany and the US needs attention. Germany is above target, while the US margin is about eight percentage points lower and should be investigated.


Customer and Product Decisions
Using customer and product results to decide what to do next

Figure 3. Customer contribution and product revenue-profitability relationship.
5. Top 10 Customers by Contribution Margin GC and Country
Rationale: Ranking customers by contribution margin shows which customers bring the most financial value, not only the most sales. The colours show each customer’s country, while the percentage beside each bar shows the change from the previous year.
Finding: Bavaria Bikes leads at about $7.40 million, followed by Capital Bikes and Rädelland. Most of the leading customers are from Germany, which supports the result in the country chart. Red Light Bikes is still in the Top 10, but its contribution fell by 8.22%, so it may need attention.
Action: Give important customers proper account support, investigate customers whose contribution is falling, and consider suitable loyalty or cross-selling offers. Any discount should be checked carefully so it does not reduce the margin too much.
6. Product Revenue versus Profitability
Rationale: The bubble chart shows revenue, margin percentage, sales quantity and product category together. The 46% line makes it easy to see which categories meet the case-study target.
Finding: Accessories have the strongest margin at about 55% and are clearly above target. Offroad Bikes are close to 46%, while Road, Touring, Trend and E-Bikes are below it. Touring Bikes have the highest revenue but are still below target, so even a small margin improvement could make a large difference. Trend Bikes have low quantity and low margin, so they need a different decision.
Action: Check supplier costs, prices and discounts for Touring and Road Bikes first. Keep Accessories performing well and consider selling accessories together with bicycles. For Trend Bikes, management should decide whether to improve the category, reduce its range or stop selling it after checking its costs and importance to the business.
Overall recommendation and limitation
The dashboard does more than summarise sales because each section points to a possible action. First, investigate the weaker US margin. Second, prepare stock and staff before the April-June sales increase. Third, maintain relationships with valuable customers and check customers whose contribution is falling. Finally, focus on high-revenue products that are below the 46% target. Industry research did not identify 46% as a standard used by bicycle companies, so it is treated as Global Bike's internal target. Possible outside reasons should only be confirmed after adding promotion, price, stock, supplier and weather data.
